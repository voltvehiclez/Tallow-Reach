# src/sim: gameplay rules

Rules of the game. Reads and changes `state`, talks to other systems with events. Imports `data/`,
`state/` and `util/`. **It holds no mesh, no material and no scene** (refactor steps 7a and 7b): an
enemy, a bullet, a missile, a crate, a spark, a rock and the player's own ship are plain records, and
`render/views.js` draws them. Step 7c removes the remaining render imports: place entry
emits `place-entered`, and missile lock reads the camera direction supplied in `state.aimDirection`
by the frame loop. Named weapon mounts come from `data/mounts.js`, transformed by `pilot.js`.
**It never touches the speakers or the keyboard:** a sound is `emit("sfx", { name })` (played by the
`SFX` table in `core/audio.js`) and the pilot's intent arrives in its `controls` record (filled for the local player by
`core/input.js`, shape in `state/controls.js`). `tests/layers.test.mjs` keeps all of that true.

| File | What it does |
|---|---|
| `flight-model.js` | THE shared flight model: `stepFlight(ship, stats, input, dt)` turns (local Y/X/Z quaternions, stick deadzone), throttles, strafes, lifts and boosts every ship in the game with the same maths. The player (via `player.js`, which keeps power, boost fuel and collision around it) and every NPC (via `npc-pilot.js`) fly through it; only the stats differ (including the per-axis `yaw`/`pitch`/`roll` multipliers and the one `turnAll` multiplier for all three axes). |
| `npc-pilot.js` | The NPC pilot adapter: turns the AI's desired velocity + aim point into the shared model's stick/throttle/strafe/lift/boost (proportional steering clamped to -1..1), so an NPC is limited by its own turn rate and top speed exactly like the player. NPC drives are unlimited today; `boostAllowed` is the hook a later power system gates. |
| `flight-instruments.js` | Read-only pocket object enumeration with injectable sensor policy, relative-velocity ballistic interception and per-step displacement speed. No saved fields. |
| `step.js` | `stepSim(dt)` runs the gameplay systems in order; `createSimClock()` feeds it 60 fixed ticks per simulated second and caps long frames. `isSimulationPaused()` is the frame gate: only the pause card and the title screen stop the clock. The starmap and the in-flight ship inventory are read-only overlays — they release flight input, but the world keeps running behind them in every mode. |
| `colliders.js` | Model-fitted triangle-mesh collision, plain data, and the one place both ships and projectiles meet it. Rocks collide via one local soup per shape (the built-in lumps replicate the drawn rock exactly, or an `asteroid.glb` overrides them); scenery colliders baked by `render/collideModel.js` register per place. **Ships:** `collideRockAt`/`collideSphereAt` sphere-vs-soup (Ericson closest point), pushed out and bounced; `SHIP_RADIUS` is the hull radius. **Projectiles:** `sweepColliders`/`segmentColliderHit`/`segmentRockHit` walk the whole segment a bolt or missile covered in one tick (Möller–Trumbore, two-sided) and return the nearest hit with its fraction `t` — a 900 m/s bolt covers 15 m a tick, so an end-point test would tunnel through thin geometry. Rocks are transformed into model space rather than every vertex out. `noclip`-named GLB nodes and `collide: false` features opt out of both, by design. |
| `player.js` | `createPlayerRecord()` creates an independent player with its own controls; `createPlayer()` places the local one in the list and binds `state.profile`. Ship setup (`setShip`), `flight(dt, player)` and `hurt(d, player)` operate on that player's own runtime and portable `profile` (`configurePlayer` sets up a non-local fit and profile; hull hits stamp `profile.hp[ship]`). A ship with no drive gets no thrust and no turn, and pressing a control warns "Equip thrusters to move". |
| `shield.js` | The ship's shields from the fitted module: `shieldOf` a loadout, `refreshShield` after a fit, ship change, load or dock, `updateShield` each frame (accepts an explicit player; defaults to local; off while being hit, then `regen` a second after the `delay` lull). Also charges the ship a flat `shieldDraw` MJ/s for keeping them up, and bleeds them away when the ship cannot pay. |
| `power.js` | Ships' power budget: `powerOf` a loadout, `refreshPower`/`refillPower` after a fit change, `updatePower` each frame, and `hasPower` to ask whether the ship can pay for a demand. `setDraw`, `hasPower` and `updatePower` accept a player or an explicit power budget; the default is the local `state.power` for the HUD; `draw` is set by the thrusters in `sim/player.js`, the shields in `sim/shield.js`, the guns and the beam in `sim/weapons.js`. |
| `hull.js` | The Hull HP the fitted hull module grants (`hull_basic`: `hp` 10), multiplied by the hull's own `hpMul` (the Test Ship's is 100) via `effectiveHullOf`, and `refreshHull()` after a fit change, a ship change, a save load or a dock. No module fitted means no Hull HP — any hit reaching the hull kills the ship. The saved fraction in `profile.hp[ship]` is applied; nothing here repairs. |
| `drive.js` | The ship's drive: `driveOf` a loadout, `refreshDrive` after a fit, ship change or undock, `hasDrive` to ask whether the ship can move at all. Writes the player's `drive = { spd, turn, draw, boost, ignite, bmax, buse, bfill }` and sizes the boost tank (`player.enMax`), which the flight model and the chase camera read; `driveDraw` works out the per-second power the pilot is asking the thrusters for, and `ignite` is the one-off slug for lighting the boost. No thruster fitted gives 0 / 0: no thrust, turn or boost. |
| `fitting.js` | Moving modules between bays (`profile.loadout`) and the current station's stock (`worldState.stationStores[placeId:featureId]`): `fitModule`, `unequip`, `storedFor`, `fittedAt`, `ownedCount`. The only writer of `profile.loadout`; refreshes power, drive, shields, hull and guns on the acting pilot after every change. |
| `inventory.js` | Where things are kept, how full each place is in m³, and how its slots are arranged. Authoritative stocks are `{ itemId: count }` (`worldState.stationStores[key]` with `worldState.store` home alias, `profile.holds[shipId]`, and open rift `CACHE`), paired with reconciled slot layouts (`worldState.stationSlots`, `profile.holdSlots`, `cache.slots`) via `slotsAt`/`slotAt`, `pickupSlot`, `takeFromSlot`, `placeHeldStack` (drop/merge/swap and right-click place-1), `returnHeldStack`, `shiftMoveSlot`, `stackAll`, `takeItemFromSlot`, `addItem`/`takeItem`/`moveItem`, `discardItem`, `buyItem` and `valueOf`. Pure and unit-tested. |
| `consumables.js` | Using an item (step 14): the right-click **Use** path. `USE_ACTIONS` maps an item's `use` field (`data/items.js`) to a handler that returns `{ text }` when it worked or a sentence when it did not; `useItem(id, where, player, profile)` runs it, consumes exactly one unit and only on success, and always returns something to show. `canUse(id)` is what the flight card asks before offering the entry. Today's one action is `spawn-rift`, which calls `openRift`. |
| `guns.js` | The guns in the primary bays: `gunsOf` a loadout (pure, one `{i, id, dmg, rate, speed, draw, barrels}` per fitted gun) and `beamsOf` (the beam modules there, with `period`/`range`/`draw`), `refreshGuns` after a fit, ship change, load or dock, and `moduleListName` — the short name for a fitted list. Keys 1 and 2 select primary bay 1 and 2 (`sim/weapons.js` `primarySlotIndex` folds legacy `pw` values); only the selected bay's module fires. Own module so `fitting.js`/`economy.js` can refresh the guns without importing the shooting code. |
| `weapons.js` | Per-player firing, mining, missile stock (`profile.msl`) and lock state; projectile flight advances once per world tick. Firing the fitted guns on left click (each gun on its own 1/`rate` cooldown, one bolt per linked barrel per shot, drawing `draw` MJ/s while the trigger is held), the mining beam (per fitted `beam_basic` module: `period` seconds of continuous contact per ore into the acting pilot's hold, `range`, resetting the moment contact stops), missiles and lock-on (per the fitted `missile_basic` module: `dmg`, `cap`, `cd`; the missile's own flight and lock mechanics come from `data/weapons.js`). Re-exports `gunsOf`/`refreshGuns`. |
| `combat.js` | `fire(..., player)` records the shooter (`owner`) and inherits their velocity; enemy bullets hit the active player they reach; kills credit the killer pilot's `profile.kills` and emit `enemy-killed { ..., player, encounter }`, and crates pull toward and enter the hold of the nearest active pilot with cargo room. Bullets, hostile kills, crates and pickups — all plain records (step 7b): a bullet `{ id, kind: "bullet", place, missionId, pos, quat, v, own, owner, dmg, l }`, a crate `{ id, kind: "crate", place, encounter, encounterType, missionId, persistent, crateSlot, pos, quat, t, item, spin }`. What a kill drops comes from the enemy's table in `data/loot.js`; mission-created shots and ordinary wave loot carry a mission tag so abandonment can remove them without clearing ordinary encounters. Authored mission drops are persistent, saved in `world.missionState` with their item, `slot` and defeat position, restored when the pocket loads, and marked collected only after a successful physical pickup. `slot` tells several crates of the same item apart, so one recovery contract can own nine crates of Salvage without them collapsing into one (`nextMissionCrateSlot` picks the next free one). Encounter crates announce their final pickup so place memory can start a cooldown. |
| `enemy-flight.js` | Per-pilot attack/strafe/break decisions, limited delayed dodges, predictive ship/rock avoidance, scenery probes and friendly firing-lane checks. Uses independent serializable brain RNG. |
| `ai.js` | Chooses the nearest active player; the generic spawner reads a place feature's encounter id and checks each active pilot's `flight.spawnT` and `profile.missions` (local pilot first). Collision: enemies are solid hulls — they ricochet off rocks (rocks damage them, credited to no pilot), off scenery (no damage, like the pilot), off each other (no damage, an AI nudge) and off the pilot (both sides take impact damage). Hostiles: `spawnEnemy(id, pos)` builds one from its def in `data/enemies.js` (name, Hull HP, colour, scale, faction, every number) and `AI_BEHAVIOURS` holds the ways they fight (`chase`: close in, then circle and shoot). Hunt contracts still select their requested enemy; one-shot patrol features become cleared place memory after their final member dies; mission-tagged waves emit `mission-trigger { trigger: "wave-clear" }` on their last kill, and mission abandonment removes their tagged hostiles, shots and loot. The record itself, and every access to "where is it, which way does it point", is `enemy.js`. |
| `enemy.js` | A hostile as **plain data** (step 7a): `{ id, kind: "enemy", encounter, def, n, f, loot, hp, mx, pos, quat, v, fire, o }`. `enemyPos`, `enemyFwd`, `turnEnemy`, `enemyDist`, `enemyVel` are the only ways to read its position and rotation; `dropEnemy(e)` takes it out of the world and emits `enemy-removed`. It holds **no mesh** — `render/views.js` draws it. |
| `loot.js` | Rolls the tables in `data/loot.js`: `rollLoot(id, rng)` returns drops `{ item, count, scatter }`, `pickLoot(id, rng)` returns one weighted item id. Pure (no screen, no state), so it is tested by rolling tables 10,000 times. |
| `asteroids.js` | The asteroid field. A rock is a record (step 7b): `{ id, kind: "rock", place, slot, memoryId, shape, pos, quat, s0, scale, r, type, ore, max }` — `s0` is its born size, `scale` its drawn size (mining shrinks it, and the collision rule reads it), `slot`/`memoryId` identify its persistent field slot. Its ore comes from the `asteroid` table in `data/loot.js`. `ROCK_SHAPE_COUNT` is how many shapes exist. |
| `encounters.js` | Builds static `derelict` and `resourceCache` features from `data/encounters.js` into crate records, and marks the feature cleared after its final crate is picked up. |
| `place-memory.js` | Owns the simulation-second cooldown records in `state.worldState.placeMemory`: mined rock slots and cleared encounter feature ids. `updatePlaceMemory(dt)` only runs from an unpaused simulation tick; expiry and `respawn-place` events emit `place-memory-ready`, so loaded places rebuild content without direct coupling. |
| `targeting.js` | Target lock (**F**): `toggleLock()`, `lockName(lock)`, `lockPosition(lock)` and `pruneLock(player)`. A lock is `{ k, o }` — a kind and the thing locked: `e` hostile, `a` asteroid, `c` crate, `s` station, and since step 14 `r` a rift door, `g` a run's way out (labelled `(sealed)`/`(open)`) and `k` its prize canister (`(locked)`/`(open)`). `pruneLock` drops a lock whose thing is gone, and every label is a sentence, never an id. |
| `economy.js` | Cargo, storage, selling, repairs, every purchase. The outpost's one purchase is `buyItem(shopId, id, qty, kind)` — items land in station inventory through `inventory.js`, while `kind: "ship"` calls `buyShip` to berth ships in the active station's Hangar (`localShips`, capped by `hangarCap` = `world.base.hangar + 1`, supporting multiple copies of the same hull). `canSellShip`/`sellShip` sell stored ships from the Hangar at 60% resale (`shipSellValue`), returning cargo and non-stock fitted upgrades to station storage when room permits. `shopCatalogue(shopId)` and `shopMaxBuy` drive the compact collapsible Shop tree and Buy-amount popover; `shopStock()`/`setShopStock()` track limited rows in session state. `sellHold()`/`sellStored()`/`sellInventoryItem()` sell goods and spare modules. |
| `contracts.js` | Facade for per-station contract offer boards; active progress lives in the generic mission runner. Each station has its own offers (`worldState.board` for home, `worldState.boards[placeId]` for others) and its own 10-minute (`CONTRACT_REFRESH_SECONDS = 600`) unpaused simulation-second refresh cooldown in `worldState.boardTimers`, ticked by `updateContractTimers(dt)` in `step.js`. `acceptContract` removes only the accepted offer; empty boards stay empty across save/load until the timer reaches `0` and `refreshBoard` is pressed. |
| `campaign.js` | Campaign-mode progress and offer rows; it keeps authored campaign definitions separate from the open-world story list, and holds the act boundaries: `campaignTravelRefusal` (a pocket needs `allowTravel` before the pilot may jump) and `campaignDockRefusal` (no docking while a destroy objective is live). |
| `campaign-act.js` | Starts, resumes and restarts one campaign act (`startCampaignAct`, `restartCampaignAct`): fresh world, fixed ship, mission at the act's own step or at a saved `checkpoint`, auto-finish → `campaign-act-completed`. |
| `campaign-flight.js` | The campaign's in-flight beats: `control` steps finish when the pilot holds the authored control, `chase` steps track the story ship's authored course (falling behind says the authored line and resets that objective, never the act), story actors take their role from the step (lead / observe / guard) and a vanished story actor is re-created from its authored `spawn-ship`. |
| `flight-course.js` | Builds a `FLIGHT_COURSES` phase into waypoints at its fixed start (`buildCourse`, `startCourseAtBeginning`) and reports progress (`courseFinished`, `courseRemaining`). |
| `missions.js` | Data-defined mission runner. Listens for kill, mine, pickup, reach, delivery, trigger, dialogue, inventory, warp-end and simulation-tick events; advances several active missions, runs story dialogue/scripts/waits/flags/branches/parallel objectives, waits for complete scripted item transfers, matches authored physical mission pickups by mission identity and flying state, retries resource-blocked handoffs after inventory changes, docking or the end of arrival warp, keeps a mission whose steps are done (`missionObjectivesDone`) as an active hand-in (`missionHandIn`) until `completeMission` consumes the exact collect/deliver/mine requirement from station stock then ship hold and pays — at the mission's own station only (`missionHandInStation`: its data's `handIn.station`, else the station that gave it out, stamped at accept time; `atMissionHandInStation` and `canCompleteMissionHere` drive the screens, and anywhere else pays nothing and says where to go), shows `handInNextText` meanwhile (never "complete"; a `delivery` contract such as mine reads as its delivery sentence from the moment it is taken and never adds a "You have 2 of 3." tally, because the station store may hold part of the load; a recovery contract instead counts only the crates it spawned — `step.owned`, matched on `payload.missionId`, so two salvage jobs each recover their own — and reports "2 out of 3 crates recovered" through `missionRecoveryText`, while any Salvage in the station or hold pays the hand-in), fails a mission whose mission-only hand-in is lost (death after the last step, or no copy anywhere: `missionLostHandIn`), applies declared failure outcomes only while objectives are open unless the mission's needed item is gone for good, words everything by `missionKindWord` ("Contract"/"Mission"), and emits progress/objectives-done/failure/reward events. Dialogue steps may explicitly require docking for station contact; leaving a sector while any dialogue is open keeps that conversation resumable instead of treating the open panel as abandonment, and world-changing scripts wait for the pilot's authored place/state requirements. |
| `session.js` | `configurePlayer(player, profile)` binds an isolated profile and copies its fit into independent transient runtime; `launch(player)` and `dock(player)` operate per pilot, only clearing transient field entities when no other active pilot remains in space; launching also tops up each open recovery contract's own crates in the field (`ensureSalvageCrates`), one per crate still to be recovered. Local `player-dead` runs ship loss/recovery — the lost hull's records go with it, and the ship you fall back to (another
owned hull, or the Wasp the station lends) is made complete with `addShipRecords`/`ensureShipRecords` before `setShip`, so it
can be fitted and flown; non-local death recovery belongs to step 16. `resumeFromSave(player)` is the one path a load takes: it puts the pilot back where `world.resume` says (docked at the station when there is no resume point) — and since slice 14b it rebuilds a saved run around them first, calling `restoreInstances()` before `enterPlace` and `populateInstances()` after the pilot is standing in it; since slice 14c it rebuilds standing simple wormholes the same way with `restoreWormholes()`. Emits `launched`, `docked`, `died`. |
| `sectors.js` | Per-pilot warp sequence (`startWarp`, `updateWarp`, `cancelWarp`): the starmap proposes an explicit destination, this module requotes the hop (free local warp or explicit gate) and its cost/requirements, then runs the unchanged charge/white-out/settle timing from `data/sectors.js`. Refusals emit `travel-denied`; the legacy no-destination call uses the same travel recheck for old headless callers. |
| `effects.js` | Cosmetic explosions and exhaust, as records: `{ id, kind: "fx", pos, v, l, t, col, size }`. Colour and size are data, so the view needs to invent nothing. |
| `places.js` | Generic place loader (`loadPlace`, `refreshPlace`, `enterPlace`), arrival/station/field helpers. Builds entities from authored `PLACE_DEFS` or generated `place-templates` features, records local system visits in compact galaxy knowledge (never for an instance, which belongs to no system), and listens for `place-memory-ready` to rebuild expired rock slots/static encounters before emitting `place-entered`. `enterPlace` names the place it puts the local pilot in as the world's current place — a runtime instance included (slice 14b), so a save made inside a run comes back inside it; `destroyInstance` names the pocket again on the way out. |
| `uncharted.js` | Persistent off-chart geography and rotating paired wormhole pockets; core odds and physical scale share the simple wormhole environment; seeded matching counts inbound and outbound doors against the same cap. |
| `galaxy.js` | Shares the seeded central-body roll with instance skies. Pure starmap resolution: `generateGalaxy(seed)` runs a replaceable organic-web generator (v2 sparse radial-depth tree; v1 retained to resolve removed legacy gate saves), applies `GALAXY_OVERRIDES`, prunes deleted-pocket links, assigns per-system travel defaults, advisory tiers and independent seeded star/black-hole cores, then indexes and validates one-peer gates and policy-aware reachability. Exposes visibility policies, pocket/link queries, `recordSystemVisit`, `systemCoreName`, `systemPocketLayout` (derived pockets orbit the visual core; author `mapPos` wins, gate dots face their peer; never saved), and since 2026-11-16 `systemScale` and `systemSpatialLayout` — the same layout lifted into AUs (map x → AU x, map y → AU z, height pinned at 0; `auPerMapUnit` × a seeded per-system multiplier) with the star/black hole at the origin and `radiusAu` as the system's outer boundary; every pocket gets a true `[x, y, z]` position, never saved, and runtime-registered wormhole pockets are included like their siblings. |
| `galaxy-session.js` | Save/world adapter: `prepareGalaxySave(raw)` resolves the saved seed, installs the live place catalog, sanitizes the save, records the current visit and recovers a removed/unknown pocket safely to home. Failed preparation restores the previous catalog. The resolved web lives in session `state.galaxy`, never in the save. |
| `travel.js` | Pure travel rules used by both the starmap and `startWarp`: `travelQuote`, `canTravel`, `travelOptions`, `findRoute`, `findRouteToSystem` (fewest executable warp hops to any pocket in a destination system), `findRouteToPocket` (exact pocket), and `findDestinationRoute` (dispatches destinations). Reads resolved galaxy policies/links, registered transport modes and requirement handlers; returns plain cost/mode/reason data. Same-system warp is free unless the system opts into `intraSystem: "links"`; cross-system gates always need their explicit link. Route search respects both policies. Non-empty costs are refused until a cost handler exists. No UI, input, audio or runtime state. |
| `wormholes.js` | Simple wormholes (slice 14c): the runtime behind `data/instances.js`'s `WORMHOLE_SPAWN` / `WORMHOLE_POCKET` / `WORMHOLE_SITE_POOL`. An always-on standing population of **75** simple wormholes in the universe, never more than **3 per system**; `topUpWormholes()` spawns them, a fresh/legacy fill staggers their clocks (lifetime rolled 0–20 minutes), and every replacement after an expiry gets the full 20 minutes. Each is a record (`id`, `parent` system, `seed`, `template`, `lifetime`) saved in `world.wormholes`; its dot on the system map is derived from its seed, the pocket is registered through `registerWormholePlace` so it hangs on that system's map and `placeAt`/`loadPlace` fill it like any other place. `updateWormholes(dt)` ages them, collapses them on time (never `kill`) and takes their doors out of normal space; the far side is a re-enterable site (`reentrant: true`) with a **Stable wormhole** out — no clock of its own — and its objective pays once per wormhole. `restoreWormholes()` rebuilds them from a save (save v21, idempotent per world object); `registerWormholePlaces(rawWorld, galaxy)` does the same inside `prepareGalaxySave` before validation. Emits `wormhole-collapsed` and `wormhole-gone`. |
| `instances.js` | Rifts and instances (step 14): the runtime behind `data/instances.js`. `createInstance(template, seed, { origin, tier })` registers a temporary place through `registerInstancePlace`, so the ordinary `placeAt`/`placeDef`/`loadPlace` path fills it, and returns its record — the same seed gives the same instance, the same rocks and the same cache. `rollRiftTemplate(riftId, rng)` is the weighted pick a rift kind makes; `riftClearance` measures `RIFT.clearance` against stations, field edges, planet surfaces and single rocks; `openRift(riftId, player)` hangs a `{ kind: "rift" }` door in the pocket; `enterRift`/`leaveInstance`/`riftAction` are the close-range **R** press in and out; `updateInstances(dt)` runs every instance's clock, warning, objective, its two objects' spin and lock state and the prompt, and `updateRifts(dt, placeId)` turns the doors and offers theirs. A run puts three things in the world: the door (`kind: "rift"`), the **way out** (`kind: "gate"`, `record.exitPos`, template `exit { n, pos, r }`) and the **prize canister** (`kind: "cache"`, `record.cache.entity`), and `instancePrompt(record, player)` is what R would do here in the run's own words. `completeObjective` unlocks the canister; `openCacheTrade(record, player, profile)` is the pilot's **R** beside it — it offers a **trade**, not a transfer: `ui/cache.js` then shows the ship's hold and the canister side by side and `tradeCacheItem(dir, id, n, player, profile)` moves cargo between them one click at a time (`dir` `"take"` or `"stow"`, `n` `Infinity` for a whole stack), through the ordinary rules of `sim/inventory.js`, whose `CACHE` container is the open canister's own stock — so nothing enters a hold by itself, a full hold loses nothing and says so, and cargo can be put back. A canister's stock is a plain `{ itemId: count }` map, which is what lets the inventory layer treat it as one more container; a take that empties it drops it from the world, and a stow brings it back. `cacheUnits(cache)` counts what is inside, and `closeCacheTrade(record)` puts the trade away. Every answer is a sentence on screen. Three registries keep the rules in data: `OBJECTIVES`, `EXIT_REQUIREMENTS` (the twin of `TRAVEL_REQUIREMENTS` in `travel.js`) and `EXPIRY_OUTCOMES` (`kill`, `collapse`). `destroyInstance(record, reason)` empties the place, puts every pilot inside it back at the spot they entered from, takes the door out of the pocket and unregisters the place; it also runs on `dying`, so dying inside despawns the rift at once, and it writes the pocket back into `world.place`. **A run is saved** (slice 14b, save v20; v21 keeps it and adds the standing wormholes): `restoreInstances()` rebuilds the records and place registrations of `world.instances` — call it before anything loads that place — and `populateInstances()` fills in the wave, the canister and the gate for every run the pilot is standing in; `resumeFromSave()` in `session.js` calls both, in that order. Natural (simple) wormhole sites leave and re-enter at any time (`leaveInstance` keeps the run for `reentrant` templates), count `mine-ore` objectives off `mined` events (a new `OBJECTIVES` kind) and never carry a clock of their own. |
| `world.js` | `clearTransient(placeId)` (docking: hostiles, shots and missiles) and `clearWorldObjects(placeId)` (warping: transient crates and sparks; persistent authored mission crates stay, and rocks stay because they are the pocket's). |
| `entity.js` | **Every entity except a hostile and the pilot's ship** goes in and out of the world here (step 7b): `addEntity(list, e)` gives it an id from `util/ids.js` and emits `entity-spawned { id, kind }`, `dropEntity(list, e)` emits `entity-removed`, `clearEntities(list)` empties one. One place, so a view can never miss one. |
| `pilot.js` | Where the player's ship is (step 7b): `playerPos`, `playerFwd`, `playerDist`, `setPlayerPos`, `facePlayer`, `playerVel`, `playerLocal`, `shipScale`. The ship is `state.player.pos` / `.quat`; `playerPos`, `playerFwd`, `playerDist`, `setPlayerPos`, `facePlayer`, `playerVel`, `playerLocal` and `playerMount` accept an explicit player (otherwise local); mounts also accept a hull id. `holdShip()` kills the local ship's carry and throttle and is what `ui/cache.js` and `ui/cargo.js` call the moment a loot surface opens, so an open card can never fly the ship into a rock. `playerMount` resolves named mounts; `playerLocal` builds a point in the hull's own frame by hand (pos + scale + quat), never from `matrixWorld`. This is how muzzles, the missile rail, the beam and the chase camera find the ship. |
| `missile.js` | `launchMissile(..., player)` can inherit the named player's velocity (defaults to local); missile flight is shared and advances once per world tick. A missile as a record `{ id, kind: "missile", pos, quat, d, sp, t, l, dmg }`: `launchMissile`, `updateMissileFlight`, and the hit that makes it explode. It homes on the hostile it locked (`t`). |

Rules:
- Use `emit`/`on` to connect systems that should not know each other.
- Never import `core/audio.js`: emit `sfx` (see `docs/HOW_TO_ADD.md` recipe 9).
- Never read `keys`/`pointer` or `core/input.js`: read the acting player's `controls` (recipe 8b).
- Never call `Math.random()`. Use `random()` from `util/rng.js`, so `setSeed(n)` makes a whole run
  repeat; `tests/rng.test.mjs` fails if `Math.random` appears in this folder.
- New rules that need numbers: put the numbers in `src/data/`.
- `docs/HOW_TO_ADD.md` recipes 3, 4, 6, 10, 11 touch this folder.

`navigation.js` implements destination navigation: `jumpRoute` recalculates the next pocket hop and calls `startWarp`, which requotes it. No destination and unavailable routes produce HUD notices; it never automatically chains warps.


Targeting accepts the nearest visible mesh hit from the render layer for every lockable kind, validates it against live candidates and prioritizes it over angular assistance. Headless calls without a rendered hit retain asteroid collision soups and other-kind sphere proxies. Session validates permanent home stations and recovers there. Ship storage is per station (`profile.shipPlaces`, v23); death recovers to a surviving ship at the home station or loans a fully fitted local Wasp without moving ships parked at other stations. `shipBaseStats` (`hull.js`) computes unfitted base Hull HP, cargo and bonuses for right-click Ship details.

Boost sets full throttle only on the ignition transition after its flat MJ debit, never on the initial press. Weapons support primary and secondary selected bay indices 0–4; selection banks are independent. A selected empty slot never falls back. Secondary switching clears acquisition but preserves the shared reload cooldown/ammo to prevent bypasses.

Weapon selection updates the flight slot HUD without emitting redundant centre-screen equipped/empty messages. Independent selection and missile-lock reset behaviour are unchanged.

Pocket row selection validates live membership, uses standard F-lock records for supported target kinds, and toggles the same target off; `isPocketRowLocked` shares the exact same matching rule so the HUD outlines the locked row across live entities and derived feature copies. Additional spatial features/projectiles use generic locks pruned on removal/place change.
